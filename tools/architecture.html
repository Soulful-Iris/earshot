the front door
earshot.servesystemd unit, 700M cap
Stdlib http server. Serves the page, takes the upload straight to disk in 1 MB chunks, reports status, serves stems. It separates nothing and now loads no model — it has to stay answering.
accepts, caps, queues
studio.pyqueue of one
25 MB, 6 minutes, ONE job at a time. Two concurrent jobs is 2.8 GB on a 1.8 GB machine, so the queue is not politeness — it is the whole reason a second upload does not kill the first.

5-second windows, 25% overlap, crossfaded and written out behind the read head. Nothing whole-file is ever in memory: a 90-minute film is 1.9 GB as float32 and this box has 1.8.

You asked if one box is the best architecture. It is not.
It is what I could reach, and a diagram makes every compromise look like a decision. The queue of one is not a design choice, it is a symptom. As of tonight the constraint is gone — iam:CreateRole is allowed as long as the role carries the account’s permissions boundary, and I ran a real Fargate task end to end to check. So a task per job, off this box, is available now and needs nothing from you.

parallel fraction -> Amdahl ceiling, any CPU~1.9×
GPU, from the literature — NOT measured by me~20×
So a 16-vCPU container buys almost nothing: only about 46% of the work parallelises. The win from moving off the box is not speed, it is that jobs stop queueing behind each other and a job can no longer kill the website. Real speed needs a GPU, and ec2:RunInstances and SageMaker are both explicitly denied — that one would be a decision for you.
Delivery cannot fail the job: S3 and email are wrapped, because the one real failure so far was a finished separation reported as a failure by the step after it. Progress is seconds done / seconds total written to a file by the worker — at 3× realtime a spinner is a lie for ten minutes at a stretch. Every box here was read off the live config tonight, not from memory.
